# SERVER 504 OPERATIONAL CODEX — V2

## Purpose
This Codex defines the practical rules used to operate Server 504 under the Server 504 Charter. It should be short enough for R4/R5 teams to consult during real disputes and flexible enough to update when game mechanics change.

---

# Chapter 1 — Internal NAP and Combat Conduct

## 1.1 Protected Internal Actions
Unless specifically authorized under this Codex, the following intentional actions against protected Server 504 players or alliances are prohibited:
- attacking or rallying bases;
- attacking protected APCs where the game mode allows player choice;
- looting same-server trucks;
- deliberately blocking or disrupting gathering;
- occupying or disrupting another alliance’s assigned strategic territory without authorization;
- intentionally causing avoidable losses to another protected 504 player.

## 1.2 Accidents and First-Time Mistakes
A first-time accidental incident should normally be resolved by:
1. direct contact between R4/R5 teams;
2. acknowledgement or apology where appropriate;
3. reasonable restitution if measurable losses occurred;
4. closure of the case once both sides confirm resolution.

Repeated “accidents,” refusal to respond, or evidence of intent may escalate the matter into a formal case.

## 1.3 Arena Etiquette
Players are encouraged to avoid unnecessary repeated attacks, deliberate rank-sniping, or harassment of other Server 504 players when reasonable alternatives are available. Arena participation remains an individual progression activity and is not subject to a blanket same-server ban unless a specific event strategy has been published.

## 1.4 Internal Military Authorization
No alliance may independently initiate organized military action against another signatory alliance. Any exceptional internal military authorization requires a three-quarters Council vote, recusal of directly involved alliances, a defined scope, and a defined end condition.

# Chapter 2 — Shared Assets, Territory, and Benefits

## 2.1 General Rule
Shared assets shall be distributed by published schedules, stable allocation rules, or measurable criteria wherever practical. “Council discretion” should be a fallback, not the default system.

## 2.2 Armory
1. The Seasonal Strategic Annex shall publish the normal Armory rotation or access method.
2. Eligibility criteria may include activity, event participation, contribution, and ability to use the benefit effectively.
3. A temporary override may be approved by a two-thirds Council vote for a defined major server objective.
4. Any override must state the beneficiary, purpose, duration, and return-to-normal date.
5. No alliance receives permanent Armory ownership.

## 2.3 Capital Benefits
1. Capital buffs shall be administered impartially by the presiding alliance according to published server priorities.
2. Capital control does not give the presiding alliance political superiority.
3. Requests should be handled through a simple published queue or category system where the game allows.

## 2.4 Territory and Mining Centers
1. Each season should begin with a published baseline allocation.
2. Allocation should consider activity, event participation, defense capacity, strategic geography, and ability to maintain the asset.
3. Minor ranking fluctuations alone do not automatically change allocation.
4. Major restructuring requires a two-thirds Council vote and a stated strategic reason.
5. Emergency battlefield changes may be temporary and should revert or be reviewed after the event.

# Chapter 3 — Server Events and Military Cooperation

## 3.1 Event-Based Operational Roles
Alliances may be assigned or volunteer for roles such as:
- Combat Core;
- Rally Lead;
- Rally Support;
- Defense;
- Point Optimization;
- Development;
- Logistics;
- Reserve.

Roles may change from event to event and do not create permanent alliance classes.

## 3.2 Server Defense Force / Event Task Force
1. For major cross-server events, the War Working Group may organize an event-specific Server Defense Force or Task Force.
2. Participation is voluntary unless an alliance has already accepted a specific seasonal commitment.
3. Players remain members of their original alliances unless they voluntarily move temporarily for a published event strategy.
4. Operational commanders coordinate battle execution but do not gain political authority over the Council.

## 3.3 Supreme Capital / Temporary Elite Alliance
A temporary elite alliance may be formed when game mechanics make concentration of top players strategically beneficial. Selection should consider power, activity, experience, rally value, discipline, availability, and team fit. Participants return to their original alliances after the event unless all parties independently agree otherwise.

## 3.4 Intelligence
Sensitive military intelligence should be shared only with players who need it for the operation. Confidentiality rules must not be used to conceal misconduct unrelated to genuine strategic secrecy.

# Chapter 4 — Conduct and Communication

## 4.1 Expected Conduct
Members are expected to communicate without harassment, personal threats, discriminatory attacks, deliberate provocation, or sustained behavior intended to destabilize server cooperation.

## 4.2 Rumors and Public Accusations
Serious public accusations should be supported by evidence. Players should be encouraged to move disputes from World Chat into direct R4/R5 contact or formal mediation before the conflict escalates.

## 4.3 Drama vs. Disagreement
Disagreement with a Council decision, alliance leader, strategy, or policy is not itself misconduct. Punishment requires an actual rule violation, not merely criticism or political opposition.

---

# Chapter 5 — Dispute Resolution

## 5.1 Three-Stage Process
### Stage 1 — Direct R4/R5 Resolution
Routine incidents should first be handled directly between the affected alliances.

### Stage 2 — Neutral Mediation
If direct discussion fails, both sides may request a neutral mediator who is not from either affected alliance.

### Stage 3 — Formal Council Case
A formal case is appropriate when:
- the matter remains unresolved;
- the violation is repeated;
- the incident is serious;
- the alleged conduct affects server-wide assets, security, or stability.

## 5.2 Evidence
Acceptable evidence may include screenshots, battle reports, relevant chat logs, video, timestamps, game records, or other verifiable material. Evidence should be limited to what is relevant to the case.

## 5.3 Conflict of Interest
Representatives from directly involved alliances must recuse from investigation and sanction voting. They retain the right to present evidence and respond.

## 5.4 Decision Summary
A formal decision should state:
- confirmed facts;
- rule applied;
- sanction or remedy;
- duration;
- vote result or threshold met;
- appeal deadline if applicable.

## 5.5 Appeal
One appeal may be requested when new material evidence is presented or a significant procedural error is demonstrated. An appeal is not a complete retrial merely because a party dislikes the outcome.

# Chapter 6 — Sanctions and Remedies

## 6.1 Principles
Sanctions must be enforceable, proportionate, time-limited where practical, and targeted at the responsible party.

## 6.2 Sanction Ladder
**Level 0 — Mediation / Restitution**  
Apology, compensation where possible, corrective action, or negotiated settlement.

**Level 1 — Official Warning**  
Recorded warning for a confirmed violation.

**Level 2 — Temporary Loss of Shared Benefits**  
Temporary loss of access to specified shared benefits controlled by the server coalition.

**Level 3 — Temporary Exclusion from Coordinated Server Activities**  
Temporary removal from specified server-run arrangements or privileges.

**Level 4 — Temporary Suspension of NAP Protection**  
Used only for serious or repeated violations and must specify exactly what protection is suspended, against whom, and for how long.

**Level 5 — KOS**  
Reserved for extreme or persistent misconduct where lesser remedies have failed or where immediate server security requires it.

## 6.3 KOS Definition
Every KOS decision must specify:
- whether it applies to a player or an alliance;
- permitted target types: base, APC, truck, territory, or other;
- start time and end time or review date;
- conditions for ending the KOS;
- whether restitution or compliance can terminate it early.

KOS against an entire alliance requires evidence that the alliance itself directed, adopted, or repeatedly protected the offending conduct. One player’s misconduct does not automatically make all alliance members legitimate targets.

## 6.4 Non-Enforceable Punishments
The Council shall not order internal demotions, forced transfers, R4/R5 appointments, or other internal personnel decisions inside an autonomous alliance. It may request action, but the alliance retains internal authority.

# Chapter 7 — 504 Council and Working Groups

## 7.1 Council Representation
1. Each eligible alliance has one voting seat.
2. Each alliance may name one primary representative and one deputy.
3. The primary and deputy may both participate in discussion, but only one vote may be cast for that alliance.

## 7.2 Eligibility
The Seasonal Strategic Annex shall define which alliances hold Council voting eligibility for that season. Criteria should be objective and may include activity, sustained membership, server contribution, ability to fulfill shared responsibilities, and stability. Eligibility should not change every time rankings move slightly.

## 7.3 Working Groups
The Council may maintain non-political working groups such as:
- War & Cross-Server Operations;
- Development & Shared Assets;
- Migration & Recruitment;
- Dispute & Conduct;
- Communications / Documentation.

Working groups may contain experts from multiple alliances. They make recommendations and execute approved plans but do not receive separate Council votes.

## 7.4 Voting
- Routine operations: simple majority.
- Territory restructuring, major benefit changes, sanctions, major diplomacy: two-thirds.
- Migration policy, Charter changes, permanent governance changes, internal military authorization: three-quarters.
- Tie: status quo remains.

## 7.5 Records
Material decisions should be recorded in a concise decision log. Sensitive operational details may be redacted until disclosure is safe.

# Chapter 8 — External Relations

## 8.1 Formal Agreements
Formal alliances, mutual-defense agreements, or long-term cross-server arrangements require a two-thirds Council vote unless they create commitments significant enough to fall under the three-quarters threshold in the Charter.

## 8.2 Allied, Neutral, and Hostile Servers
The Seasonal Strategic Annex shall maintain the current list of:
- allied / partner servers;
- neutral or undeclared servers;
- hostile or priority-target servers.

The previous Codex listed Server 502 as a current priority partner. That status should be reconfirmed during ratification of the new Seasonal Annex rather than hard-coded permanently into this Codex.

## 8.3 Unofficial Diplomacy
Players may communicate socially with other servers, but no individual may make commitments on behalf of Server 504 without authorization.

## 8.4 Provocation
Unnecessary cross-server provocation that creates strategic cost for Server 504 may be addressed when evidence shows a material impact. Casual trash talk alone should not automatically become a Council case.

---

# Chapter 9 — Migration, Recruitment, and Alliance Health

## 9.1 Migration Plan
Each season or migration window should include a published internal plan covering:
- manpower gaps;
- desired player profiles;
- seat allocation principles;
- alliance placement needs;
- newcomer integration;
- responsibility for external recruiting communication.

## 9.2 Alliance Health
Alliance health should be assessed using several indicators, including:
- active member count;
- event participation;
- leadership continuity;
- online coverage;
- rally capability;
- recruitment capacity;
- ability to hold assigned territory or roles.

No fixed Total Alliance Power threshold automatically forces a recovery plan.

## 9.3 Recovery Support
An alliance showing sustained decline may request or be offered:
- recruitment support;
- temporary player exchange for specific events;
- leadership mentoring;
- strategic role adjustment;
- voluntary merger facilitation.

## 9.4 Mergers
Mergers remain voluntary. The Council may facilitate negotiations but cannot compel an alliance to dissolve or merge.

---

# Chapter 10 — Review and Amendment

1. The Codex should be reviewed when game mechanics, server population, or recurring disputes show that a rule no longer works.
2. Codex changes normally require a two-thirds Council vote, unless the change modifies a Charter-level principle, in which case the Charter amendment threshold applies.
3. Season-specific schedules, lists, targets, and assignments should be changed in the Seasonal Strategic Annex rather than in this Codex.
